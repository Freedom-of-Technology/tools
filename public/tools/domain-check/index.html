About records files

What a records file is

Your domain's DNS records are a short list of settings kept by your DNS company: where your website is, which service gets your email, and the codes that let other services prove they work for you. A records file is that list, saved as plain text. Save one while everything works, and you can always see what changed, and put it back.

Saving one from Cloudflare

  1. Sign in to Cloudflare and click your domain.
  2. Open DNS, then Records.
  3. Click Import and Export, then Export.
  4. The file lands in your Downloads folder. Keep it somewhere safe.

Saving one from GoDaddy

  1. Sign in to GoDaddy and open your Domain Portfolio.
  2. Click your domain, then open DNS.
  3. Open the Actions menu and choose Export Zone File.
  4. The file lands in your Downloads folder. Keep it somewhere safe.

Another DNS company? Look in its DNS settings for "export" or "zone file". Most offer one.

What each result means

Matches
What's live is the same as your file. Nothing to do.
Different
What's live isn't what your file says. If you or someone helping you changed it on purpose, save a new records file. If nobody did, change the record at your DNS company back to the value in your file. Just changed it? Lookups can show the old answer for a while; the result says how long.
Missing
Nothing is live for this record. If nobody removed it on purpose, add it back at your DNS company with the value in your file. A brand-new record can take up to an hour to show up.
Cloudflare answers
The record has Cloudflare's orange cloud on, so Cloudflare answers with its own addresses and where it really points can't be seen from outside. It's there and working.
GoDaddy website
GoDaddy's website builder answers for it. It's there and working.
Copy line differs
Only in files kept by FreeOT. A note in the file that writes a long value out in one piece, for copying, doesn't say the same as the record under it. One of them is wrong.
Which DNS company runs your domain
The registry, where your domain is listed, says a different DNS company runs it than your file does. If you moved it on purpose, save a new records file. If you didn't, someone may have changed your domain: get help right away.
DNSSEC
A lock on your records that some DNS companies turn on. The registry and your file disagree about whether it's on. If nobody changed it on purpose, get help.
Not checked
A kind of record this page can't look up, or names that are live but aren't in your file. DNS doesn't list every name a domain has; your DNS company's export does.
Couldn't check
The lookup didn't work this time. Try again in a minute.
