Your Google profile, in plain words

Why it matters
Your Business Profile is how you show up on Google Search and Maps: your hours, phone number, photos and reviews. Whoever is its primary owner can change any of it, and can take everyone else off it, you included. A profile set up by a web designer, an agency or an employee often stays in their name long after they've moved on.
Where to look
Sign in to the Google account you use for the business, and go to business.google.com. In your profile, choose More, then Business Profile settings, then People and access. It lists everyone who can manage the profile, and what each of them can do. Google renames its menus from time to time; its own help page on owners and managers has the current steps.
Primary owner, owner and manager
  • Primary owner: one per profile. Only the primary owner can make someone else the primary owner.
  • Owner: full control. Owners can add and remove people, change the profile, and remove it.
  • Manager: can do most of what an owner can, but can't add or remove people or remove the profile. The right choice for anyone who helps you with it.
The 7-day wait
Google makes anyone newly added as an owner or manager wait 7 days before they can remove other people, or be made the primary owner. So moving a profile into your name takes two steps, a week apart: first you're added as an owner, then you're made primary owner. Google: transferring primary ownership
The common trap
An agency, a web designer or a former employee as primary owner. You may be able to edit your profile today, but they can take you off it, and only they can make you primary owner. Google's rules say anyone who manages a profile for a business must hand ownership back as soon as the business asks. Google: ownership guidelines
Asking Google for ownership
If whoever holds your profile won't hand it over, or can't be reached: go to business.google.com/add, find your business, choose Request access, and fill in the form. Google emails the current owner, who has 3 days to answer. If they say no, you can appeal. If they don't answer, a way to claim the profile may appear, though not always. A business without a shop or office contacts Google support instead. Google: requesting ownership
Agreements and open bills
If you have an agreement with whoever holds your profile, such as an agency, it may say what's handed over to you, and when. Check it before you ask. This page can't tell you what your agreement means.

What this page sends, and to whom

Nothing. Once this page has loaded, your answers and your result stay in your browser, and the page doesn't contact FreeOT, Google or anyone else.

What it doesn't do

  • It doesn't look anything up, and never signs in to Google. It never asks for your business's name, anyone's name or any login. "Who holds the profile?" is a choice such as "A web designer or agency", never a name.
  • It doesn't send your answers anywhere. When you see your result, your answers are added after the # in the page's address, so you can share it; browsers never send that part to the website.
  • It has no tracking scripts or cookies, and stores nothing. Opening the page loads it from FreeOT's site, like any web page.
  • It can't connect anywhere even if it tried: its security settings tell your browser to block every connection the page itself might make.

Is this page the same as the code on GitHub?

It's meant to be, file for file: there's no build step, so the site serves the files in the public repository as they are, and every change is made there, where its history is public. Two ways to check:

  1. Right-click this page, choose View page source, and compare it with public/tools/google-profile-owner/index.html on GitHub. That one comparison covers everything the page runs: the source lists a fingerprint of every script and style it uses, and your browser refuses to run any file that doesn't match its fingerprint. Up-to-date browsers check them; an older one skips the check.
  2. Watch what it sends. In Chrome or Edge, press F12 (on a Mac, Command+Option+I), open the Network tab, then see your result. You'll see nothing go out.
