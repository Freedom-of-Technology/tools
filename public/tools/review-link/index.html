Using your code

Getting your review link
  1. On a computer, sign in to the Google account that manages your business, and open your Business Profile: search Google for your business's name, or go to business.google.com.
  2. Choose Read reviews, then Get more reviews.
  3. Google shows your review link. Choose the copy button next to it, and paste the link into the box above.
Google offers this on a computer only, not in its phone app. No Business Profile yet? Google's help explains how to add your business.
Try it first
Point your phone's camera at the code on your screen. Your phone should offer to open Google, at the review box for your business. Every code this page offers has already passed a test: the page scans it back and checks it holds exactly your link.
Which file to use
  • Counter card, 5x7: your business name, your logo and the code, sized for a 5x7 inch photo print. Print it and stand it on your counter.
  • Code, SVG: for anything else printed, such as receipts, flyers, menus and signs. It stays sharp at any size; send this one to your printer or designer.
  • Code, PNG: for documents, slides and email.
Printing the counter card
Order it as a 5x7 photo print at a drugstore or photo counter, or print it at home on 5x7 photo paper. It fits any 5x7 picture frame or sign holder. Scan the printed card with your phone before you put it out.
Other places for your link
The link works anywhere people can tap it: your thank-you emails and texts, your receipts and invoices, and your website. Use Copy link once your code is made. For printing on something else, keep the code at least 2 cm (about 3/4 inch) wide, with the white border around it.
Google's rules for asking
Ask every customer, not only the happy ones, and never offer anything in return: no discount, gift or entry into a drawing. Google removes reviews it thinks were paid for, and it can restrict a business that offers rewards. Reviews from your own staff aren't allowed either. Customers need a Google account to leave a review. Google's rules.
Why it never expires
Many QR code websites make "dynamic" codes: the code holds a link to their server, which then sends people on. When the subscription ends, the code stops working, often after it's printed on everything. This code holds Google's own link, so it works as long as your Business Profile does. Google makes the link; this page only puts it in a code.
Why only a link from Google
A code on your counter is trusted: customers scan it without reading where it goes. So this page takes only a link to Google, and says so if what's pasted goes anywhere else. Google has a few kinds of review link, all of which work here.
Your logo
Any size works. A large logo, such as one from your designer or a photo, is more than a card needs, so your browser makes a smaller copy of it and every file you download uses that copy. Your own file isn't changed.
A logo in the middle
QR codes carry spare information so they still read when part is scuffed or covered. With your logo in the middle, the page uses the most spare information a code can carry, keeps the logo small, and offers the code only if it still scans. Because it's small there, a fifth of the code's width, small words and thin lines in your logo won't show; your counter card shows it larger. A busy or very dark logo can still make a code harder to read on older phones, so try it before printing.

What this page sends, and to whom

Nothing. Once this page has loaded, it makes your code, your counter card and your files in your browser, and doesn't contact FreeOT, Google or anyone else.

What it doesn't do

  • It doesn't upload your link, your business name or your logo. Your browser reads the logo from your computer or phone to draw it; the downloads are made in your browser, too.
  • It doesn't check your link with Google. Try your link opens it in a new tab only when you choose it, just as if you'd pasted it into your browser.
  • It has no tracking scripts or cookies, and stores nothing. Opening the page loads it from FreeOT's site, like any web page.
  • It can't connect anywhere even if it tried: its security settings tell your browser to block every connection the page itself might make.

Is this page the same as the code on GitHub?

It's meant to be, file for file: there's no build step, so the site serves the files in the public repository as they are, and every change is made there, where its history is public. Two ways to check:

  1. Right-click this page, choose View page source, and compare it with public/tools/review-link/index.html on GitHub. That one comparison covers everything the page runs: the source lists a fingerprint of every script and style it uses, and your browser refuses to run any file that doesn't match its fingerprint. Up-to-date browsers check them; an older one skips the check.
  2. Watch what it sends. In Chrome or Edge, press F12 (on a Mac, Command+Option+I), open the Network tab, then make a code. You'll see nothing go out: what's listed is your own browser making the downloads, at addresses starting blob: or data:.

The QR code is made with qrcode-generator and test-scanned with jsQR, two widely used free libraries, copied into the repository as published.
